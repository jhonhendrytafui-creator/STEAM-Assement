# STEAM EDP Rubric Suite (C2–C5) — Revised

A coordinated set of four rubrics that measure the **Engineering Design Process (EDP)** across one student-group project. Each rubric uses a **4-level Likert scale** (1 Beginning · 2 Developing · 3 Proficient · 4 Exemplary) and is grouped by **Dimension → Indicator**.

---

## How the four rubrics fit together (read this first)

**EDP coverage**

| Rubric | EDP Phase | Source of evidence | The "lens" — what this rubric judges |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| C1 | (Gateway) | Abstract | Is the idea worth approving? *(already in use — not revised here)* |
| **C2** | **Ask + Research** | Google Doc | Do they *understand* the problem and the science? |
| **C3** | **Imagine + Plan** | Google Doc | Did they *generate and plan* a justified solution? |
| **C4** | **Create + Test + Improve** | Logbook / Journal | Did they *execute and iterate* like engineers? |
| **C5** | **Communicate** | Live presentation + Q&A | Can they *explain and defend* the work? |

**The single thread that links all four.** The **Success Criteria** (the measurable targets and constraints) are *defined* in C2, *planned toward* in C3, *tested against* in C4, and *defended* in C5. If you enforce nothing else, enforce this thread — it is what turns four separate scores into one coherent story.

**The double-counting trap (important).** STEAM understanding and the problem statement appear in every rubric. Do **not** re-grade the same thing four times. Use the lens column above:
- C2 grades whether they *understand* the science.
- C3 grades whether their *plan correctly applies* it.
- C4 grades whether the *actual build used* it.
- C5 grades whether they can *communicate and defend* it.

**Scoring across rubrics.** The four rubrics have different totals (C2 = 32, C3 = 40, C4 = 48, C5 = 40 after revision). Convert each to a **percentage** before combining, so no phase is silently weighted more just because it has more indicators. Suggested EDP weighting (tune to taste): C2 20% · C3 25% · C4 35% · C5 20% — the build/iterate phase carries the most weight because it is the heart of the EDP.

## C3: Imagine & Plan — Revised

**Lens:** Did the group generate *several* ideas, choose one *for good reasons*, and produce a *buildable* plan with measurable targets?

**What's new vs. your draft:** added **Dimension I (Ideation & Solution Selection)** — your draft jumped straight to one chosen solution, but the "Imagine" half of the EDP is exactly the part that was missing. Also added a **Success Criteria & Test Plan** indicator so the plan defines *how they will know it works* before they build.

| Dimension | Indicator | Level 1: Beginning | Level 2: Developing | Level 3: Proficient | Level 4: Exemplary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I. Ideation & Selection** *(NEW)* | **1. Divergent Ideas** — Did the group brainstorm several different solution ideas before choosing? | Only one idea is shown. No brainstorming is visible. | Two ideas are mentioned, but the second is not real (a "throwaway" option). | Three or more genuinely different solution ideas are described. | Many distinct ideas are explored, including at least one bold or unusual approach, showing real creative range. |
| | **2. Convergent Choice** — Did they choose using clear criteria, not just "the easiest one"? | No reason for the choice, or "because it's easy." | A reason is given, but it is opinion-based and ignores the Success Criteria. | The chosen idea is compared against the others using the C2 Success Criteria. | Uses a structured method (e.g., a decision matrix scoring each idea against weighted criteria) to justify the winner objectively. |
| **II. Execution & Planning** | **3. Materials & Budget** — Is there a complete parts list with a realistic budget? | No materials or budget listed. | Some materials listed; budget missing or unrealistic. | Comprehensive parts list with a realistic budget. | Exhaustive list with a detailed, priced budget that respects the C2 cost constraint. |
| | **4. Timeline & Tasks** — Is there a clear, sequenced timeline of who does what, by when? | No timeline or task breakdown. | Vague timeline; tasks not assigned. | Clear, sequenced timeline with tasks assigned to group members. | Realistic timeline with dependencies and milestones, showing genuine engineering project management. |
| **III. Design Spec & Success Criteria** | **5. Visual / Blueprint** — Is there a diagram, sketch, or blueprint that matches the plan? | No visual provided. | Visual is messy or does not match the written plan. | Clear visual that maps to the plan. | High-quality labeled blueprint/diagram that a stranger could build from. |
| | **6. Measurable Success Criteria & Test Plan** *(NEW)* — Did they define how they will *test* whether the prototype works? | No success criteria; "it should just work." | States a vague goal ("it should be good") with no test described. | Lists measurable targets and a basic plan to test them. | Defines precise, measurable targets tied to the C2 constraints **and** a clear procedure for testing each one. |
| **IV. Risk & Contingency** | **7. Risk Assessment & Plan B** — Did they predict specific problems and prepare backups? | Ignores risk; assumes perfect execution. | Only generic risks ("it might break"); no real Plan B. | Identifies realistic risks with basic mitigation ideas. | Sharp, specific risk foresight with a strong, actionable Plan B for each. |
| **V. STEAM Application in the Design** | **8. Planned Application** — Does the *design* explicitly require STEAM concepts to function (not just decorate)? | STEAM concepts are absent or decorative. | The design leans on one discipline; others are bolted on. | The design plan applies 1–2 STEAM concepts to make the solution function. | The design *requires* multiple STEAM disciplines working together; remove one and the plan fails. |

## C4: Create, Test & Improve (Logbook) — Revised

**Lens:** Does the journal show a *true history* of building, failing, testing, and improving — as a team?

**What's new vs. your draft:** added **Dimension III (Testing & Evidence)** so "Test" in the EDP is explicit (results and data, not just "it broke"), and **Dimension VI (Collaboration & Contribution)** because this is group work and the logbook is your best defense against free-riding. Your "Depth of Task Description" dimension is kept.

| Dimension | Indicator | Level 1: Beginning | Level 2: Developing | Level 3: Proficient | Level 4: Exemplary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I. Structure & Consistency** | **1. Organization & Dates** — Is the log dated, ordered, and easy to follow? | Incoherent; few entries, no dates. | Disorganized; missing dates or large gaps. | Clear structure; most dates present; logical flow. | Professional log: every work session dated, ordered, and instantly readable. |
| **II. Problem-Solving Loop** | **2. Failures & Fixes** — Does it document specific problems and the *steps* taken to solve them? | Only successes shown; no struggle. | Problems listed without solutions or detail. | Problems and their follow-up solutions are recorded. | Each failure is analyzed (*why* it failed) with the full iteration path from problem to fix. |
| | **3. Plan Fidelity** — When they deviated from the C3 plan, did they record *why*? | No reference to the original plan. | Deviations happen silently. | Notes when the build differed from the plan. | Explains every deviation with a clear engineering reason for the change. |
| **III. Testing & Evidence** *(NEW)* | **4. Tests & Data** — Do they record actual tests with results or measurements? | No testing; assumes it works. | Mentions testing vaguely; no results. | Records tests with basic results against the Success Criteria. | Logs structured tests with real measurements/data and compares them to the C2/C3 targets. |
| | **5. Evidence-Based Decisions** — Do test results *drive* the next change? | Changes are random or cosmetic. | Changes are made, but not because of test data. | Test results clearly motivate at least one design change. | A documented loop of *test → measure → decide → change* recurs throughout the build. |
| **IV. Reflection** | **6. Honest Self-Assessment** — Is there genuine reflection on struggle and growth? | None. | Superficial ("today went well"). | Recognizes progress and key learning moments. | Deep, honest reflection balancing struggles, achievements, and what they learned as makers. |
| **V. Depth of Task Description** | **7. Descriptive Precision** — Do entries say *exactly* what was done? | Missing or just phase names. | Vague/repetitive ("did research," "worked on it"). | Clear entries specifying the work done, with minor gaps. | Vivid, precise descriptions leaving zero ambiguity about each session's technical work. |
| **VI. Collaboration & Contribution** *(NEW)* | **8. Division of Labor** — Does the log show who did what and how the team coordinated? | No sign of who did anything. | Work appears done by one person, or roles are unclear. | Entries show tasks shared across named members. | Clear, balanced contribution from all members, with coordination visible across entries. |
| | **9. Team Decision-Making** — Are disagreements or joint decisions documented? | None. | Decisions appear with no discussion. | Records that the team made key decisions together. | Documents how the team debated options and resolved disagreements to reach decisions. |

## C5: Communicate (Presentation & Q&A) — Revised

**Lens:** Can the group *communicate* their EDP journey clearly and *defend* it under questioning? (Content quality is already scored in C2–C4; here you judge how well they convey and defend it.)

**What's new vs. your draft:** **Q&A is pulled out into its own dimension (V)** — you flagged Q&A as central, but your draft buried it inside "Delivery." I also reframed the content dimensions toward *communication of* the work (not a re-grade of the work) and added a **limitations / next-steps** indicator. Your separate "10-question generator" system prompt still works perfectly as the *tool* a teacher uses to source Q&A questions — this rubric is what the teacher *scores* with.

| Dimension | Indicator | Level 1: Beginning | Level 2: Developing | Level 3: Proficient | Level 4: Exemplary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I. Problem Framing** | **1. Story & Significance** — Do they make the audience care about the problem and its target user? | Problem unclear; no audience. | States the problem and audience flatly, no "why it matters." | Frames the problem clearly and explains why it matters to a specific group. | Opens with a compelling hook that makes the problem's significance vivid and undeniable. |
| **II. Explaining the Solution** | **2. How It Works** — Can they explain the mechanism (Input → Process → Output) to an audience? | Treats the solution as "magic." | Says what it does, not how. | Walks through the logic of how the solution works. | Crystal-clear technical walkthrough an outsider could follow. |
| | **3. STEAM Reasoning** — Do they explain *which* STEAM concepts make it work and how they connect? | No STEAM concepts named. | Names concepts but not how they connect. | Explains how 1–2 STEAM concepts drive the solution. | Shows the disciplines working together, with clear interdependence. |
| **III. Evidence of the EDP Journey** | **4. Showing the Process** — Do they present iterations, failures, and tests (not just the polished result)? | Shows only the final product. | Mentions "we changed some things" with no detail. | Shows at least one real iteration with its before/after. | Presents the full journey — failures, tests, and improvements — as proof of engineering thinking. |
| | **5. Limitations & Next Steps** *(NEW)* — Do they honestly state what's still weak and what they'd do next? | Claims it is perfect / ignores limits. | Vague ("we could improve it"). | Names a real limitation and a next step. | Sharp, honest critique of their own work with a credible improvement roadmap. |
| **IV. Delivery & Visual Aids** | **6. Participation & Timing** — Is speaking time shared and well-paced? | One person dominates; poor timing. | Uneven sharing; rushed or overlong. | Reasonably even participation and pacing. | Seamless hand-offs; every member contributes; tightly timed. |
| | **7. Visual Aids & Demo** — Are slides/model/demo clear and supportive? | Missing or cluttered. | Visuals present but distracting or hard to read. | Clear visuals; prototype shown. | Polished visuals and an effective live demo that strengthens the message. |
| **V. Q&A Defense** *(ELEVATED)* | **8. Depth Under Pressure** — Can they answer unscripted, challenging questions with real understanding? | Cannot answer; freezes or guesses. | Answers surface-level; struggles with follow-ups. | Answers most questions correctly and with reasons. | Answers confidently with evidence, even on tough, unexpected questions. |
| | **9. Intellectual Honesty** — Do they handle the limits of their knowledge well? | Bluffs or invents answers. | Deflects or hides gaps. | Admits when unsure. | Admits unknowns gracefully and proposes how they'd find the answer. |

## Notes for the AI grader (applies to C3, C4, C5)

Keep your existing system-prompt wrapper for each (Role, Input Expectation, single-paragraph casual-but-sharp output). Update only two things to match this revision:

1. **Dimension count and max score** per rubric (C3 = 8 indicators / 32 → categorize at /32; C4 = 9 / 36; C5 = 9 / 36). Recompute your category bands proportionally, e.g. Exemplary ≈ 85%+, Proficient ≈ 65–84%, Developing ≈ 45–64%, Beginning < 45%.
2. **Feedback must reference the Success-Criteria thread** — e.g. "your logbook never tests against the under-$10 target you set in C2," which is the kind of sharp, specific critique that makes the whole suite cohere.
